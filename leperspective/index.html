The Story of News

How we went from shouting around a fire
to doomscrolling at 2 a.m.

A very short history of how humans share what happened. Keep scrolling →

01
~40,000 BCE

Oral Traditions

News was a story told out loud. Around fires, at markets, over walls. If you missed it, you missed it. The original "breaking" news broke and then disappeared into memory.

Range: shouting distance
🔥
02
~3,200 BCE

Early Scribes

Then someone pressed a reed into wet clay and froze a fact in time. Scrolls, tablets, hand-copied manuscripts. Slow. Expensive. Read by maybe twelve people. Honestly, peak signal-to-noise.

Range: one careful courier
📜
03
1440

The Print

Gutenberg hits "print" and the world never shuts up again. Pamphlets, papers, headlines. Suddenly the same fact lands in a thousand hands on the same morning. The first feed.

Range: a whole city
🗞️
04
1950s

The TV

News got a face, a desk, and a dramatic theme song. The whole country watched the same thing at 9 p.m. and believed roughly the same things by 9:30. Reach: enormous. Nuance: optional.

Range: the living room
📺
05
Now

Social Media

Everyone's a broadcaster. Everything is breaking. THIS CHANGES EVERYTHING, the future is here, every 17 minutes. Infinite reach, infinite outrage, infinite "thread 🧵". You are very tired.

Range: the entire planet, at once
📱
06
Today, ~1 min
